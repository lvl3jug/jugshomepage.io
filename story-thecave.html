The Cave

Ever since I was a boy I loved to explore.

Most of my early years were spent hiking the Appalachian trails with my mother and father, catching fish in the rivers to cook on an open fire that father had made while mother looked for cave openings. She always loved caves.

Sure enough, mothers love of caves was passed down to me, something about the vastness of them never failed to draw me in.

The tunnels that would stretch for miles under the earths crust.

Most times we would delve in, take photographs, pocket small stones or rocks to inspect in the sunlight and guess what the cave was made out of, or even how it was made in the first place.

That day was cold. I remember that much.

We had been hiking for hours, I can’t remember exactly how many but if I had to guess I would put it roughly around 5. We were in an area I had never seen before. Judging from fathers face, he had never been there either. Mother was unphased, jolly even.

As father placed his pack on the ground I let my eyes dart the surroundings. A large clearing with a beautiful waterfall cascading down a cliff to our north. My compass, a present I had been given for my ninth birthday, showed me we had walked east along the bottom of the cliff until we had reached the clearing. It was an arduous journey for my now twelve year old legs, but they weren’t quite ready to give in yet.

Mother had taken to her usual routine of scouting our perimeter for wild vegetation and of course, cave openings.

I had placed my pack beside fathers and taken a seat next to him, the both of us staring intently at the waterfall, captivated by it.

“Son,” father breathed out gently as he spoke, unbuckling the latches on his pack after a second of pondering.

“Your legs are red.”

“I’m okay father.” I couldn’t feel them before he had mentioned, but as I looked down I noticed a stinging sensation around my ankles and calves.

“It must have been stinging nettles on the trail.”

Father huffed and stood on his feet, quickly looking around for dock leaves to crush into a paste.

“Boys!”

A voice echoed through the trees, maybe 10 or so meters away. Mothers.

“Come and look at this!”

My head swivelled in her direction. I could hear the joy behind her words, she had surely found something interesting hidden in the brush. A cave more than likely.

Father looked up at the treeline briefly before turning to me.

“Go and have a look, I’ll see what I can do with these leaves.”

I put my hands on my knees and pushed myself to my feet, turning towards where mothers voice had come from before pushing forward through the brush.

My eyes scan the tree trunks and ground flora as I try to pinpoint where mothers voice had originated from, I reached down to scratch my left ankle before trying to call out to her.

“Mother!”

...

“Mother!?”

...

Something was wrong...

At that moment I could feel fear welling in my stomach, my eyes darted from tree to tree. Father was out of earshot or he would have shouted of us both, mother must be out of earshot or she would have replied.

Where is she?

I jogged forward deeper into the trees, looking left and right periodically for any sign of her. The fear in my stomach had reached my chest by now, acid was starting to burn at my throat. I swallowed it down and shouted again.

“Mother!”

Something grabbed me by the shoulders and span me around, the fear in my stomach had risen to my mouth as I began to let out a scream before realising who had grabbed me.

“Come and look at this.” Mothers eyes locked with mine and I could see the excitement behind them, I hoped she couldn't see the fear in my own.

“What is it?” I said, pushing the fear back down to my stomach and shaking it off slightly. “A cave?”

Mother laughed and let go of me before pointing towards the cliff side.

My eyes followed her finger and settled on a small hole nestled between two bushes, concealed just enough that if mother hadn't pointed it out I would have missed it entirely.

“Want to take a look?” She grinned.

I scratched my left ankle and looked back at her before looking again at the hole. Something was telling me it was a bad idea, that we should go back and tell father what we had found before we go in, but before I could reply mother had already begun to move her way through the bushes and climbed halfway inside.

“It's dark, but it looks like there's a larger room just further in. I see reflections on the ceiling so there must be some kind of water source!” She shouted back to me, the sound echoing on the inside walls of the cave. She climbed inside.

I stepped toward the hole and peered in to confirm what she described, a room maybe 20 meters high with sharp rocks jutting out of the ceiling in strange angles. Something I had never seen before.

Water sat in the middle of the room, still, reflecting the light from outside onto the ceiling making the sharp rocks appear like they were dancing, almost. Mother standing at the edge looking into the water like she had been hypnotised by the pool.

I pulled away from the hole, turning to the direction I had come from. Wondering if father knew where we were somehow. If maybe he would come looking for us if we were gone for too long.

Once again I looked into the hole, mother must have gone deeper as I couldn't see her anymore. Perhaps concealed by the darkness that the outside sun couldn't reach.

I wiped my hands on my shorts and climbed in, wondering where she must have gone.

Once inside my eyes adjusted to the darkness quickly. The left wall was some sort of limestone or marble, the right side was too dark to tell but I guessed it would be more of the same.

The fear in my stomach had returned, quietly threatening to swallow me lest I swallow it first. My eyes scanned the ceiling, the sharp rocks were slightly clearer now, too thin and uniform to be natural I thought.

“Come here.” Mother whispered from the end of the room, hidden by the darkness.

I reached into my pocket to find the matches father had given me the day before and struck one against the paper. A flame burst out of the end and almost burned my fingers before I repositioned it between them.

I looked up again, the sharp rocks weren't just on the ceiling, they were lining the walls too. They looked to be made of material different to the rest of the cave, and they didn't appear to be embedded into the rock itself, it looked to be hanging there by some form of twine or rope.

I could see the shape of something at the back of the room, it looked like mother but it didn't look quite right. The proportions were larger in places than they should be. Her arms and legs slightly too long, her head too large.

“Come here.” Mother whispered again, quieter this time.

“What's back there?” I said, trying to make the feeling in my chest back down, unsuccessfully.

“Something beautiful.”

My chest began to hurt, my heart had been thumping hard but I hadn't realised it before. Now it wasn't possible to ignore.

I could taste copper in my mouth, and my ears had started to ring slightly.

I looked towards the back of the room, I realised no matter how much I told my feet to move they wouldn't listen to me. The feeling that I shouldn't be here was front and center now, barking at me like a hungry dog.

I willed myself to step forward before I heard something familiar outside of the cave.

“Son!?”

I turned to the mouth of the cave, I hadn't realised how far inside I had travelled but it seemed like it was an eternity away. The sunlight outside barely touching the area I was standing.

It was fathers voice...

And mothers.

The acid in my throat threatened to jump out of my mouth, my heart thumping as I turned toward the back of the cave.

Something was standing there, hunched over with its back to the ceiling. Smiling at me unnaturally with its pointed teeth lit only by my dying match.

I dropped the match and turned, breaking into a sprint. I hit the ground with my left foot, then my right, before tripping and slamming into the wall of the cave. One of the sharp rocks embedded into my arm.

I jerked my arm quickly, snapping the rock from the wall and ran for the entrance again. I could hear the thing behind me, stomping slowly as I reached the mouth of the cave and threw myself out, gulping in the outside air. I hadn't even realised I was crying but my face was wet with a mixture of sweat and tears.

“Father!” I screamed, vomit escaping with the last syllable, soaking the ground flora.

I pulled myself to my feet and ran in the direction I had come.

When I reached the clearing father and mother were there, shocked at my appearance they ran over, father reaching me first.

“Son... Your arm!” He bellowed, “What happened!?”

I looked down to see the rock that had embedded itself into my arm was not a rock at all, it was pale white and marked with dry, dark red splotches and deep scratches that looked almost like claw marks.

“You can see the bone...” Mother noted, look in fathers direction before her eyes settled on my arm again.

“It's not mine,” I gasped, breathing heavily.

“There's something else out there.”

THE END
